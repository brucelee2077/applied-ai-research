1 · WHAT IS ITWhat is researcher-to-researcher outreach?✓
🧭
What this assumes: you built a custom ThunderKittens kernel and ran an ADRS sandbox in Phase 4–5. No prior outreach experience needed.

The task: skip the hiring manager, email the paper's author

Monday you emailed generic hiring-manager inboxes. Tuesday you emailed one specific leader. Today's task is narrower still: you email the exact principal investigators (PIs) and research scientists who wrote the papers you studied and built on — the ThunderKittens paper, the ADRS paper ("Barbarians at the Gate"), FunSearch, RuleFlow, and EvoPrompt. A PI is the senior researcher who leads the lab and is usually the paper's last author.

This is not the same task as Monday or Tuesday. It has one extra requirement: the email must prove you read their specific paper and extended their specific system — not just that you are a strong general candidate.

Why this works, structurally

Research scientists do not hire off resumes. They hire off demonstrated technical fluency in their own subfield. A generic recruiter email gets triaged by HR before a PI ever sees it. But an email that says "I re-implemented your ThunderKittens tile primitive for kernel X and reproduced a measured speedup, here is the repo" goes straight into the PI's inbox as a peer signal — because it proves you can read their paper, understand a specific design decision (like ThunderKittens' register-tile abstraction over raw CUDA, or the ADRS loop's five components: Prompt Generator, Solution Generator, Evaluator, Storage, Solution Selector), and extend it.

Your leverage here is concrete, not aspirational: the Pallas MoE kernel and ThunderKittens kernel from Phase 4 (documented in Week 18), and the ADRS sandbox you built and ran for 12 hours in Phase 5 (Weeks 21–22). The Chinchilla-style scaling-law derivation and the Addition Transformer from Phase 3 are supporting evidence of research rigor — background, not the lead artifact.

So what's today's catch?

The catch is precision, not creativity. Every email must name the exact paper and one exact technical detail from it — a named figure, algorithm, or component — inside the first two sentences, before any personal framing. A PI skims the first two lines; if there is no specific paper reference and a concrete artifact link right there, the email reads as a mass-mailer and gets deleted, no matter how good the work further down actually is.

2 · INTUITIONA demo tape mailed to the actual musician✓

Sending your portfolio to a general "AI industry contacts" list is like mailing a demo tape to a record-label intern. Sending it to the exact researcher whose paper you studied is like mailing that same tape directly to the musician who inspired it.

📼➡️🏢
Generic list = noise

A record-label intern gets hundreds of tapes a week. Yours is one of many. No one on the list knows your specific reference point, so nothing you say lands.

📼➡️🎸
Direct-to-musician = signal

If you mail Jonny Greenwood a cover and say exactly which guitar tuning trick of his you reverse-engineered and how you changed it, he listens — because you are speaking his technical language about his own work.

In one line: a PI ignores generic enthusiasm the same way a musician ignores generic fan mail, but both pay attention the moment you show you understood and extended one specific thing they made.

⚠️
Where the analogy breaks: a cover song is imitation, and that's fine for music. Your outreach is not allowed to be imitation. If you just reproduce the TK kernel or the ADRS loop exactly as published, it reads as fandom, not a research signal. You have to show you extended or applied their system to something new.

4 · HOW & WHYThe rules of researcher outreach, and why they matter✓

The rules

  • Build the target list from bibliographies. Pull every author name from the ThunderKittens paper, the ADRS ("Barbarians at the Gate") paper, and cross-reference the FunSearch, RuleFlow, and EvoPrompt author lists for overlap.
  • Match the lead artifact to the paper cluster. Send the TK kernel repo to ThunderKittens-adjacent authors. Send the ADRS sandbox repo to ADRS/FunSearch/RuleFlow/EvoPrompt-adjacent authors. Never send the wrong artifact to the wrong cluster.
  • Freshness check. For each target, find their current lab and one public statement (blog, talk, tweet) from the last 12 months, so the email is not stale.
  • Cap the first wave. Start with the smallest set of highest-fit targets — the actual paper authors — before expanding to secondary citations.
✅
Sentence 1 names the paper. Sentence 2 names one figure, algorithm, or component from it. Sentence 3 states what you built, as a fact with a number.
Everything a PI needs to decide "read on" or "delete" has to be visible before any personal framing.

Why it works

  • Verifiable, not aspirational — a repo link and a measured number can be checked in under a minute; "I am passionate about your work" cannot be checked at all.
  • Speaks the recipient's own technical language — naming their exact figure or component proves you read the paper closely enough to find its actual design decision, not just its abstract.
  • One fixed follow-up window — wait 5–7 business days, then send exactly one short follow-up that adds new information (a finished metric, a fixed bug), never a repeated ping.
🔗
Causal chain: Phase 4–5 artifacts (TK kernel, ADRS sandbox) → matched to the exact paper cluster that inspired them → named in sentence one and two of the email → read as a peer signal instead of a mass-mailer.
