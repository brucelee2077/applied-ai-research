Module 22b · Generate · Day 5

Latent Diffusion& Text Control

This is the finale — the recipe behind Stable-Diffusion-class image makers. Two big ideas join here. First, run diffusion inside a small VAE latent (from Module 22a) instead of on giant raw pixels, so it is far faster. Second, steer the picture with words: turn a text prompt into an embedding, feed it to the denoiser, and mix its "with-text" and "without-text" guesses to push the picture toward the prompt. That last trick is classifier-free guidance.

🎯
By the end, you'll be able to: explain why diffusion runs in a VAE latent for speed, describe text conditioning with CLIP/T5 embeddings, and say what classifier-free guidance does (mix conditional and unconditional predictions to steer).
1Diffusion in a small latent, steered by wordsWhat is it
🧭
What this assumes: Days 1–4 — the forward/reverse diffusion loop and ε-prediction training. From Module 22a — a VAE squeezes an image into a small code (a latent) and can rebuild the image from it.

The speed problem

Doing diffusion on raw pixels is slow. A 512×512 color image is nearly 800,000 numbers, and you run the network hundreds of times to make one picture. That is expensive. The fix from latent diffusion is to shrink the problem first.

Idea 1 — diffuse in the latent

Use the VAE encoder to squeeze the image into a small latent — say 64×64 instead of 512×512, dozens of times fewer numbers. Run the whole forward/reverse diffusion there. At the end, the VAE decoder turns the finished latent back into a full picture. Same diffusion, tiny playground, big speedup.

Idea 2 — steer with text

To make "a red bicycle", you turn the words into numbers with a text encoder like CLIP or T5. That gives a text embedding. You feed this embedding into the denoiser at every step, so its noise guesses lean toward the prompt. This is conditioning.

Idea 3 — push harder toward the prompt

To make the prompt really stick, you use classifier-free guidance. The model makes two guesses — one with the text, one without — and you push the result further along the "with-text minus without-text" direction. Turn that push up and the picture follows the prompt more strongly. Keep going 👇

2Painting in a small sketchbook, then enlargingIntuition
📓
The tiny sketchbook

Painting a huge mural is slow. So you sketch the whole idea small, in a pocket sketchbook — same picture, far fewer strokes. When the sketch is right, a machine blows it up to full size. Latent diffusion does this: it does all the hard work in a tiny latent, then the VAE decoder enlarges it into a full image.

🗣️
An order to the painter

You also tell the painter what to draw: "a red bicycle at sunset." Those words become an embedding the model can read. And if you want the bicycle to really show up, you say it louder — you emphasize the difference between "with the order" and "no order at all." That emphasis is classifier-free guidance.

In one line: latent diffusion paints in a tiny sketchbook (the VAE latent) for speed, follows a spoken order (the text embedding), and turns up the volume on that order with classifier-free guidance.

Why does painting small still give a good final picture? Because the VAE latent is not just a shrunken image — it keeps the meaning: shapes, colors, layout. The decoder learned how to expand that meaning back into sharp pixels. So the diffusion model only has to get the meaning right, and the decoder handles the fine detail.

⚠️
Where the analogy breaks: a real sketch and its enlargement are the same drawing scaled up. Here the latent is a learned code, not a small picture — you cannot look at it and see the image. Only the trained VAE decoder can turn it back into something you can see.

4Classifier-free guidance, worked out by handMechanism & why

Step 1 — in plain words

At each denoising step the network makes two noise guesses. One uses the text prompt (the conditional guess). One uses no prompt at all — a blank input (the unconditional guess). The difference between them points in the "make it match the words" direction. You take the no-prompt guess and push it further along that direction by a factor called the guidance scale. A bigger push makes the prompt show up more strongly.

Step 2 — the formula

🧮
ε_text = network(x_t, t, prompt) # with the words
ε_none = network(x_t, t, blank) # without the words
ε_guided = ε_none + w · ( ε_text − ε_none )
# w = guidance scale (the volume knob)

Symbols: ε_text = the noise guess when the prompt is given. ε_none = the guess with no prompt. (ε_text − ε_none) = the direction that pushes toward the prompt. w = the guidance scale, a number like 1 (no push) up to 7 or more (strong push). ε_guided = the final noise used in the reverse step. When w = 1, ε_guided equals ε_text (plain conditioning). Bigger w exaggerates the prompt direction.

Step 3 — a worked example on one pixel

Say for one pixel the two guesses are ε_text = 0.9 and ε_none = 0.5, and the guidance scale is w = 3:

🔢
direction = ε_text − ε_none = 0.9 − 0.5 = 0.4
ε_guided = ε_none + w · direction
         = 0.5 + 3 · 0.4
ε_guided = 0.5 + 1.2 = 1.7 # pushed well past the with-text guess

With w = 3 the guided guess (1.7) is pushed far beyond the plain with-text guess (0.9), so the prompt's influence on this pixel is tripled. If you set w = 1, ε_guided would just equal ε_text = 0.9 — normal conditioning, no extra push.

Why this design wins

  • Latent = speed: running diffusion on a 64×64 latent instead of 512×512 pixels is dozens of times cheaper, which is why big models are affordable to run.
  • One adjustable knob: the guidance scale w lets you dial prompt-faithfulness at generation time, no retraining. Low w = more varied and natural; high w = closer to the words but can look over-saturated.
  • "Classifier-free": you get strong steering without training a separate classifier — the same denoiser just runs twice (with and without the prompt).
🔗
The big picture: latent diffusion + text embeddings + classifier-free guidance is the exact recipe behind Stable-Diffusion-class systems. You now understand, end to end, how a sentence becomes a picture. Next up is the review gate to lock it in.
