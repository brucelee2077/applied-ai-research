Module 4 · Represent · Day 2

Query, Key, ValueThe Three Roles Every Word Plays

Attention lets each word look at the other words to understand context — the word "bank" means something different near "river" than near "money." To decide who to look at, every word's embedding is turned into three vectors: a Query, a Key, and a Value.

🎯
By the end, you'll be able to: explain what Q, K, and V each represent, and compute them from an embedding with three matmuls.
1Three vectors from one embeddingWhat is it
🧭
What this assumes: M4 Day 1 (words are embedding vectors) and M1 Day 4 (matmul, x @ W). Nothing new needed.

Why three vectors?

Attention lets each word pull in information from the other words. To do that, each word needs to (a) say what it's looking for, (b) advertise what it offers, and (c) provide content if chosen. Those are the three roles:

  • Query (Q) — what this word is looking for.
  • Key (K) — what this word offers, to be matched against.
  • Value (V) — the content this word passes on if attended to.

How are they made?

From the same embedding x, using three separate learned weight matrices: Q = x @ W_Q, K = x @ W_K, V = x @ W_V. Three matmuls (M1 Day 4). One input, three roles. Keep going 👇

🏭
A real one: Llama 3's biggest model runs 128 separate Query heads per layer, each with its own learned projection — the same Q/K/V split you're about to build, just at industrial scale.
2Searching a libraryIntuition
🔎
Query = your search terms

What you're after. You'll compare it against every book's label to see which ones are relevant to you right now.

🏷️
Key = the label · Value = the book

Each book advertises a Key (its spine label). Where your query matches a key well, you read more of that book's Value (its actual contents).

In one line: match your query against everyone's keys, then take more of the values whose keys matched best.

⚠️
Where the analogy breaks: every word is simultaneously a searcher (Q), a book on the shelf (K, V), and does this for all words at once — not one reader at a time. Today we just build Q, K, V; the matching + mixing is the next two lessons.

4The projections — and why they're the heart of a transformerMechanism & why

The mechanism

  • Input: the embeddings x, shape (seq_len, d_model) — one row per word.
  • Three learned matrices project them: Q = x @ W_Q, K = x @ W_K, V = x @ W_V, each producing shape (seq_len, d_k).
  • A word's relevance to another = its query · the other's key (a dot product). Higher = attend more. (That full score matrix is the next lesson.)

Why a frontier lab cares

Q/K/V is the heart of every transformer. It's what lets a model resolve "bank" from context, link a pronoun to its noun, or connect a question to its answer. Those 128 Query heads from the teaser above each get their own learned W_Q slice — but Llama 3 shares just 8 Key/Value heads among all of them, a memory-saving trick you'll meet on Day 4. The projection matrices are a big share of a model's parameters, and computing Q @ Kᵀ is one of the matmuls that dominate its FLOPs (M1 Day 4).

🔗
Remember this chain: embedding x → three learned projections → Q (what I want), K (what I offer), V (what I give) → query·key scores decide who to attend to → mix the values. You've now built the pieces attention runs on.

The staff lens — one silent failure, one trade-off

The projections look like plumbing, but they are the whole point. A staff engineer knows how skipping them fails quietly, and what the three separate matrices actually buy.

⚠️
Failure mode (silent): Q and K are the same. Skip the learned projections and feed the raw embedding as both Q and K (or tie W_Q = W_K). Shapes are fine, so nothing crashes — but now each token's strongest match is itself, and attention can't form asymmetric links (a pronoun can't preferentially look back at its noun). The model trains, just weakly. A senior engineer catches this in code review by confirming Q and K come from separate learned projections.
⚖️
Trade-off: three projections vs. fewer. Separate W_Q, W_K, W_V cost parameters and compute, but they let a word ask for one thing (query), advertise another (key), and carry a third (value). Tie or drop them to save parameters and you lose that freedom. This is the exact axis GQA / MQA later trade along — a design-review decision about memory vs. expressiveness.
