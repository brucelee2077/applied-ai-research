Module 4 · Represent · Day 3

Attention Scores & Softmax
From Matches to a Weighted Blend

You have Queries, Keys, and Values. Now the payoff: match every query against every key to get scores, turn each row of scores into weights that sum to 1 with softmax, and use those weights to blend the Values. That blend is attention — the whole formula in one line.

🎯 By the end, you'll be able to: compute attention scores as Q @ Kᵀ, apply softmax to get weights, and produce the output as a weighted average of the Values.
1 · WHAT IS ITScores, softmax, weighted sum✓
🧭
What this assumes: M4 Day 2 (Q, K, V), M1 Day 4 (matmul), and exponents from M1 Day 5. Nothing new needed.

Step 1 — scores

Take every word's query and dot it with every word's key: scores = Q @ Kᵀ. This gives a grid where entry (i, j) is "how much should word i attend to word j." Just matmul (M1 Day 4).

Step 2 — softmax

Softmax turns each row of scores into weights that are positive and sum to 1 — an "attention budget" spread across the words. Bigger score → exponentially bigger share.

Step 3 — weighted sum

Multiply those weights by the Values: output = weights @ V. Each word gets a blend of the other words' Values, weighted by relevance — a new, context-aware vector. The full formula: softmax(Q @ Kᵀ / √d_k) @ V. Keep going 👇

2 · INTUITIONSplitting a budget of attention✓
💯
Softmax = share out 100%

Each word has 100% of attention to give. Softmax turns raw match scores into percentages — more to the words it matches well, less to the rest, always adding to 100%.

🧪
Output = a weighted mix

Blend the Values by those percentages. If "bank" gives 80% of its attention to "river", its new vector is mostly river's Value — so "bank" now means the riverside kind.

In one line: score every word, softmax the scores into a 100% budget, then mix the Values by that budget.

⚠️
Where the analogy breaks: it's not a person choosing — it's a smooth, differentiable formula, so the training loop (M3) can improve it. And the score grid is seq × seq, which is why long sequences get expensive (the "cost of attention").

4 · MECHANISM & WHYThe formula, the scale, and the n² cost✓

The formula

🧮
Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax( Q @ Kᵀ / √d_k ) @ V

Why divide by √d_k? With large vectors, the dot products get big, and softmax of big numbers becomes almost all-or-nothing (it "saturates"), which stalls learning. Dividing by √d_k (the square root of the key dimension) keeps the scores in a sane range so softmax stays smooth.

Why a frontier lab cares

This is the transformer operation. Two facts fall straight out of it: (1) Q @ Kᵀ and the final @ V are the big matmuls that dominate attention's FLOPs — exactly what FlashAttention speeds up. (2) The score grid is seq × seq, so cost grows with the square of the sequence length — the reason long context is expensive and a whole research area exists to fix it.

🔗
Remember this chain: Q @ Kᵀ → scores → ÷√d_k → softmax → weights (sum to 1) → @ V → a context-aware vector per word. That single formula, stacked and repeated, is what a transformer is.

The staff lens — one silent failure, one trade-off

The formula is only half the job. A staff engineer also knows the one way it breaks without telling you, and the one price you pay to use it at all.

⚠️
Failure mode (silent): softmax on the wrong axis. Write softmax(scores, axis=0) (down the columns) or over the whole grid instead of axis=-1 (across each row), and it still sums to 1 — so nothing crashes and the loss looks plausible. But each query no longer gets its own distribution over the keys, so the attention is quietly meaningless. This is the bug a senior engineer catches in code review, precisely because it never throws an error.
⚖️
Trade-off: the seq×seq score grid. Computing Q @ Kᵀ in full is exact and perfectly parallel — every token pair scored at once, ideal for a GPU. The price is O(n²) memory and compute: double the sequence length and the grid quadruples. That is the cost a staff engineer raises in a design review — it decides whether your model reads a whole book or just a paragraph, and it is exactly what FlashAttention and long-context research exist to soften.
