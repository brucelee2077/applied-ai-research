Module 22c · Generate · Day 3

Consistency
Models

Flow matching made the path from noise to data nearly straight, so you can cross it in a few steps. Consistency models ask the boldest version of the question: what if you could jump straight to the end in a single leap? They are trained so that any point along a path — no matter how far along — maps to the very same endpoint. Learn that, and you can generate a full image in one step, or a few. This one-step magic is why frontier image and video models feel instant. This is the module finale.

🎯 By the end, you'll be able to: explain a consistency model as a function trained so every point on a path jumps to the same endpoint, say why that gives one- or few-step generation, and connect it to why frontier image and video systems use it.
1 · WHAT IS ITA function that teleports to the endpoint✓
🧭
What this assumes: from Day 2 you know a path runs from noise to data, and a model can follow a velocity field step by step. Today you learn how to skip the walking entirely and land on the endpoint in one leap.

The problem: even a few steps is still steps

Flow matching cut the trip from noise to data down to a handful of steps. That is fast, but "a handful" is still more than one. For serving millions of users, or for generating video where every frame counts, people want the fewest steps possible — ideally just one.

The idea: learn the endpoint directly

A consistency model is a function that takes any point on the path and outputs where that path ends — the clean data. It does not ask "which small step next?"; it asks "where does this whole path end?" and answers in one shot.

The key rule: consistency

The name says the trick. The model is trained so it is consistent along a path: a point near the noisy start and a point near the data end must both map to the same endpoint. If every point on a path agrees on the destination, then from any single point — even pure noise — one call lands you at the data.

Why one step is possible

Because the function already knows the endpoint from anywhere, generation is just: sample noise, call the function once, done. That is one-step generation. You can also take two or three steps to sharpen quality a little. This near-instant sampling is why frontier image and video systems build on it. Keep going 👇

2 · INTUITIONA teleport that skips the whole journey✓
🚶
Walking the road

Flow matching walks the straight road from Noise to Data. It is fast because the road is straight, but you still take steps — a few footfalls to get there.

✨
The teleport

A consistency model installs a teleport pad. Stand anywhere on the road — the start, the middle, near the end — press the button, and you appear at Data instantly. No walking. One press, you arrive.

In one line: a consistency model is a teleport button that sends you straight to the endpoint from wherever you are on the path, so one press turns noise into a finished sample.

The word "consistency" is the promise the teleport makes: press the button from the start, from the middle, or from near the end, and you always land on the exact same Data spot. Every point on the road agrees on the destination. That agreement is exactly what training teaches, and it is what makes one press enough.

⚠️
Where the analogy breaks: a real teleport is perfect and free. A consistency model is learned, so the one-step jump is very good but not flawless — that is why some setups take two or three presses to sharpen the result. It also has to be taught the destinations first, either by copying a slower teacher model (distillation) or by training from scratch.

4 · MECHANISM & WHYThe consistency rule, worked out by hand✓

Step 1 — in plain words

Call the model f. Give it a point on the path and a time, and it returns where that path ends. The rule that trains it: if two points sit on the same path — say one at time t=0.9 (near noise) and one at time t=0.5 (further along) — then f must give the same answer for both. Once f obeys this everywhere, you can feed it pure noise and it returns the finished data in one call.

Step 2 — the formula

🧮
f(point_a, t_a) = f(point_b, t_b) = data
# if point_a and point_b are on the same path
sample = f(noise, t=1) # one call → finished data

Symbols: f = the consistency model. point_a, point_b = two spots on the same noise-to-data path. t_a, t_b = their times along the path. The training goal makes both outputs equal to the same data. At generation time you call f once on fresh noise and get a sample.

Step 3 — a worked example

Take one path where the data endpoint is 8. Look at two points on it (using single numbers to keep it simple):

🔢
point near noise: f(2.6, t=0.9) = 8
point midway: f(5.0, t=0.5) = 8
point near data: f(7.4, t=0.1) = 8

all three agree → generation = f(noise, t=1) = 8

Different inputs (2.6, 5.0, 7.4) sit on the same path, and f maps every one of them to 8 — the endpoint. Because they all agree, you never have to walk the path: from the noisy start, one call to f already returns 8.

Why this is a big deal

  • One- or few-step generation: one model call turns noise into a finished sample; a couple more calls can sharpen quality when needed.
  • Real-time feel: cutting hundreds of steps to one is what makes frontier image and video models feel instant and cheap to serve.
  • Built on the family: consistency models learn the endpoints of the very paths flow matching and diffusion define — the whole module builds to this.
🔗
