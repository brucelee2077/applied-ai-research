Drag the head size and watch attention collapse onto one word

What am I looking at? One word is choosing between three others. The left bars are the scores that go into softmax. The right bars are the shares softmax hands back — how much attention each choice actually gets.

What to do: drag d_k (how many slots each Query and Key has) to the right. Nothing about the three choices changes — only the number of slots. Then flip the ÷ √d_k switch and watch the shares come back to life.

What to notice

  • Drag d_k from 1 to 256 with scaling OFF. The three choices never change — but the scores grow like √d_k, and the top share climbs to 100%. The other two get about 0%, and so does their share of the learning signal.
  • Watch learning grip as you drag. That is roughly how much the top share can still move when the model nudges its weights. Near 100% confidence there is almost nothing left to grip, so this layer stops learning who to listen to — while the loss curve looks perfectly calm.
  • Now switch ÷ √d_k ON. The scores snap back to the size they had at d_k = 1, at every head size, and the shares spread out again. Same three choices, healthy blend.
  • Set d_k = 1: √1 = 1, so the switch changes nothing. Scaling only earns its keep once d_k grows — and in real models d_k is 64 or 128.

The scores here are drawn in standard steps: at the start of training the slots of a Query and a Key are small random numbers, so a dot product over d_k slots has a typical size of √d_k standard steps. That is the growth ÷ √d_k cancels. A trained model can still choose to make a score large on purpose — scaling sets the starting size, not a ceiling.
