C ≈ 6ND and the Training Footprint

Two numbers every ML-systems engineer keeps in their head: how many FLOPs (floating-point operations) it takes to train a model, and how much memory that training actually eats. Move the sliders and watch both.

What am I looking at?

This page has two calculators. Part 1 estimates how much compute (raw math work, measured in FLOPs) it takes to train a model — using the simple rule that each parameter costs about six FLOPs for every token of training data. Part 2 estimates how much memory that same training needs, and stacks the four things you must keep on the chip at once: the model weights, their gradients, the Adam optimizer's extra buffers, and the activations. Drag the sliders in each part. The numbers, the chart, and the "fits / needs sharding" verdict all update as you move them.

Part 1 · Training compute: C ≈ 6ND

A rule of thumb: training a dense transformer costs about six FLOPs per parameter, per token seen.

C  ≈  6 · N · D   ( 6 = 2 FLOP per multiply-add × [1 forward + 2 backward] )

N = number of parameters  ·  D = number of training tokens. Inference is forward-only, so it costs about 2ND instead of 6ND.

What to notice

  • Training compute is ≈ 6ND; inference (forward only) is ≈ 2ND. The factor 6 = 2 FLOP/multiply-add × (1 forward + 2 backward passes). Both scale linearly in params and tokens — double either and you double the bill.
  • Adam optimizer state alone is 2× the model size (one buffer for momentum, one for variance). So weights + gradients + Adam ≈ 4× the weight bytes before any activations — the number people forget and then hit "out of memory".
  • When the stacked bar passes the budget line, one device can't hold it: you shard the optimizer/gradients/weights (FSDP), and past that you split each layer across devices too (tensor parallel + FSDP).
