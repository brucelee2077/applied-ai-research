The Copy Machine

Two lists of numbers have different sizes. Watch how NumPy copies the small one to fit the big one — so you never write a loop.

When shapes do not match, NumPy copies a side that has size 1 so both sides line up. Then it adds them, one spot at a time. This copying is called broadcasting.

The 3 rules

A shape is just the size of each side of an array. Line up the two shapes from the right. Look at each pair of numbers:

  1. ✓ Same number? Great — they already fit. 3 and 3
  2. ✓ One of them is 1? That side gets copied (stretched) to match the other. 1 and 3 → copy to 3
  3. ✗ Two different numbers, and neither is 1? NumPy stops with an error. 2 and 3 → ✗ error

The single row never really gets stored many times — NumPy only pretends to copy it, which saves memory. But picturing the copy is exactly the right way to understand what happens.
