Gradient Descent Playground

What am I looking at? The curve is a loss — a bowl that measures how wrong the model is for each value of a weight w. Training wants to reach the bottom. The ball sits at the current w. Each "step" reads the slope (the gradient) and slides the ball a bit downhill. The learning rate sets how big each slide is. Try a tiny rate, a good rate, and a too-big rate to see what changes.

What to notice

  • The gradient points uphill, so we step against it: w ← w − lr × dL/dw. The steeper the slope, the bigger the raw pull toward the bottom.
  • The learning rate is the step size. A good rate walks down the bowl and settles at the minimum. A tiny rate barely moves — it crawls and needs many steps.
  • A too-big rate overshoots the bottom and lands higher on the far wall. Each step then jumps farther out and the loss grows — the run diverges.
