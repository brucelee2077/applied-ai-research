KV-Cache Growth & MHA vs GQA vs MQA

When a transformer generates text one token at a time, it stores the keys and values of every past token so it never recomputes them. That store is the KV cache. This page lets you feel how big it gets — and why sharing K/V heads (GQA, MQA) is the trick modern models use to keep it small.

The formula

KV_bytes = 2 × n_layers × n_kv_heads × head_dim × seq_len × batch × bytes

the 2 is for K and V · n_kv_heads is what MHA / GQA / MQA change:
MHA: n_kv_heads = n_query_heads  ·  GQA: n_kv_heads = n_query_heads ÷ group_size  ·  MQA: n_kv_heads = 1

What to notice

  • The cache grows linearly with sequence length and with batch size. Double the context, double the cache. This is why long-context serving is expensive.
  • At long context and large batch, the KV cache can dwarf the model weights — watch the line cross the 70B-weights marker. Now the cache, not the weights, decides how many requests fit in memory.
  • MQA and GQA shrink the cache by having many query heads share a few K/V heads. That is why modern models (Llama, Mistral, Gemma) use GQA: it cuts KV memory and bandwidth with almost no quality loss.
