Activations · saturation

Where an activation stops learning

What am I looking at? The solid curve is the activation f(z); the dashed curve is its slope f′(z) — how much the output moves when you nudge the input. Learning happens through that slope, so wherever the dashed curve sits near 0, the neuron is "asleep" and barely learns. Drag the marker into the flat tails and watch the slope collapse.

What to notice

  • Sigmoid / tanh: the slope is biggest near z = 0 (sigmoid tops out at 0.25, tanh at 1.0) and flattens to almost 0 in both tails. A neuron pushed far out is saturated — its slope vanishes and it stops learning.
  • ReLU: the slope is a clean step — exactly 1 for z > 0 (no shrinking, so gradients survive deep stacks) and exactly 0 for z < 0.
  • That 0 is ReLU's failure mode: a unit whose input stays negative has slope 0 forever — a dead unit that can never recover. The flat regions are where learning goes to sleep.
