Break the recipe — and watch "random" stop being random

This is the lesson's own hand-sized recipe: one hidden number, one fixed rule, and a stream of numbers that looks random but is not. Change the rule and you can wreck it in three different ways — freeze it, trap it between two numbers, or make every draw the same. Untick the seed and you break something else instead: the ability to ever get that run back.

Your recipe, and your seed

f(s) = (5 × s + 3) mod 16
Seed (the very first hidden number) 1
Multiply by (the scrambler) 5
Then add 3

Two runs of the same script, one under the other. A tick means both runs gave the same number at that step.
Where this toy stops being the truth: NumPy's real generator is called PCG64 and its hidden state is astronomically larger than the 16 values here, so it never repeats in any run you will ever do. The idea is exactly this one though — a hidden number, a fixed rule, and a stream that replays whenever you start it in the same place. These digits come from the recipe above, not from NumPy, so do not go looking for them in your own output.
