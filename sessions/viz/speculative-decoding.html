Speculative Decoding: Draft & Verify

A tiny fast model guesses the next few tokens; the big slow model checks all of them in one pass and keeps every guess up to the first mistake. Same output as normal decoding — often 2–3× faster.

What am I looking at?

  • Normal decoding makes one token per slow forward pass of the big model. That is the speed limit we want to beat.
  • Here a small fast draft model guesses the next k tokens. The big target model then checks all k guesses in a single pass and keeps every guess up to the first wrong one, plus one correction token.
  • The sliders let you change how many tokens the draft guesses (k), how often the draft agrees with the target (α), and how cheap the draft is (c). The first figure draws one round; the second figure shows how the speedup changes with k.

Speedup vs. speculation length k

The curve rises, peaks, then falls. Small k barely helps; large k wastes draft work when α is low. The dot marks your current k.

What to notice

  • One forward pass, many tokens. The target verifies all k drafted tokens in a single parallel pass, breaking the one-token-per-forward lock of normal autoregressive decoding.
  • Speedup lives and dies on α. The gain depends on how often the cheap draft agrees with the target. High α → big win; low α → a large k just burns draft compute you throw away.
  • It is exact. The accept/reject rule is designed so the kept tokens are distributed exactly as if the target had sampled them directly — no quality loss, only speed.
