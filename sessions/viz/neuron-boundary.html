Where a neuron draws its line

What am I looking at? A neuron computes z = w·x + b and fires when z ≥ 0. On a 2-input plane that "fires vs. quiet" split is exactly a straight line. Move the weights and watch the line rotate; move the bias and watch it slide. Notice what happens to the line when the bias is 0.

What to notice

  • The weights w set the line's orientation (they point straight into the "fires" side). Change w₁ or w₂ and the line rotates.
  • The bias b slides the whole line toward or away from the origin. It is the only control that moves the line off the origin.
  • Set b = 0 and the line is pinned through the origin forever — no rotation can move it away. That is why a neuron without a bias loses the freedom to lean "fire" or "quiet" independent of the inputs.
