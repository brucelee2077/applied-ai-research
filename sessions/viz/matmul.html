Matrix Multiply, Cell by Cell

What am I looking at? Three grids of numbers. On the left is matrix A, next to it matrix B, and on the right their product C = A × B. Move the sliders to change the shapes, then hover or tap any cell of C. The row of A and the column of B that make that one number light up, and the sum that builds it appears below. This single operation is what almost every neural network spends its time doing.

What to notice

  • Inner dims must match. A is m × k and B is k × n. The two k's in the middle have to be equal, or the product does not exist. Change k and watch A's width and B's height move together.
  • Each C cell is one dot product. Cell C[i][j] takes row i of A and column j of B, multiplies them position by position, and adds the results: a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + …. That sum has exactly k terms.
  • C is (rows of A) × (cols of B). The output shape is m × n — it keeps A's row count and B's column count. The shared k disappears; it is summed away inside every cell.
