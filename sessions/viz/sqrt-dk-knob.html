Turn the knob — and watch attention collapse onto one word

What am I looking at? The word bank is choosing between three other words. The top bars are the scores that go into softmax. The bottom bars are the shares softmax hands back — how much attention each word actually gets.

What to do: the knob starts OFF. Drag d_k (how many slots each Query and Key has) to the right. Nothing about the three words changes — only the number of slots. Then set the knob to ÷ √d_k, and finally to ÷ d_k to see the over-correction.

Scores in, shares out

What to notice

  • With the knob OFF, drag d_k from 1 to 256. The three words never change — but the scores grow like √d_k, and the top share climbs to 100%. The other two get about 0% — and so does their share of the learning signal.
  • Watch room left to learn as you drag. That is roughly how much the top share can still move when the model nudges its weights. Near 100% there is almost nothing left to grip, so this layer stops learning who to listen to — while the loss curve looks perfectly calm.
  • Now set ÷ √d_k. The scores snap back to the size they had at d_k = 1, at every head size, and the shares spread out again. Same three words, healthy blend.
  • Then try ÷ d_k. Too much: the scores are squashed towards zero, the shares flatten to roughly a third each, and the lead over runner-up collapses. Plenty of grip, nothing to grip about — attention can no longer tell the best match from the off-topic one.
  • Set d_k = 1: √1 = 1, so the √d_k knob changes nothing. Scaling only earns its keep once d_k grows — and in real models d_k is 64 or 128.

The scores here are drawn in standard steps: at the start of training the slots of a Query and a Key are small random numbers, so a dot product over d_k slots has a typical size of √d_k standard steps. That is the growth ÷ √d_k cancels. A trained model can still choose to make a score large on purpose — the knob sets the starting size, not a ceiling.
