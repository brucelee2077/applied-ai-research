Why divide the scores by √d_k?

The attention formula has a small, odd-looking division: scores ÷ √d_k, where d_k is the size of a key vector. Skip it and the model breaks in a specific way. This page lets you turn the scaling on and off, and grow d_k, so you can watch softmax collapse onto one option — and then recover.

What to notice

  • Set d_k = 64, scaling OFF: the raw scores are ~√64 = 8× bigger, and softmax hands almost 100% to the top option. The other options get ~0% — and so do their gradients. Learning stalls.
  • Now flip scaling ON: dividing by √d_k cancels that growth, and the weights spread back into a sensible blend. Same options, healthy distribution.
  • Set d_k = 1: √1 = 1, so scaling does nothing and the two views match. Scaling only matters as d_k grows — which it always does in real models (d_k is 64, 128, …).
One trap to avoid
The fix is not "make the scores smaller so the numbers look nice." It is that dot-product magnitude grows like √d_k purely from adding more dimensions. √d_k scaling cancels exactly that growth, so softmax behaves the same whether a head is 16-dimensional or 128-dimensional.
Quick check (3 questions)
1. What happens to softmax if the scores are very large?
It saturates: nearly all weight goes to the single biggest score, the rest get ~0, and their gradients vanish. The model can't learn to blend.
2. Why √d_k specifically, not some other constant?
A dot product of two d_k-dimensional vectors grows in magnitude like √d_k. Dividing by √d_k cancels that exact growth, keeping scores stable at any head size.
3. At d_k = 1, does scaling change anything?
No. √1 = 1. Scaling only starts to matter once d_k is larger than 1.
