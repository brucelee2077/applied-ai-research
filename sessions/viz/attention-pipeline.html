Attention · Represent

From two words to a context vector

Attention is one formula: softmax(Q · Kᵀ / √d_k) · V. It looks scary as a whole, so let's build it one stage at a time on the tiniest example — two words, “cat” and “sat”. Step through it, and on the matmul stages, hover a result square to see the exact row and column that produced it.

Stage 0 / 4

What to notice

  • A score is just one dot product. On the scores stage, each square is one query row times one key row — hover it and watch exactly those two light up. That is all “Q · Kᵀ” means.
  • Softmax turns scores into a budget. After ÷√d_k and softmax, each row of weights sums to 1.00 — a 100% attention budget spread over the words.
  • The output is a blend of Values. The final matrix is the weights used as volume knobs on the V rows. A word's output now carries context from the words it attended to.
One trap to avoid
The scores matrix S = Q · Kᵀ is seq × seq — it grows with the square of the sequence length. That n² grid is the source of attention's memory and compute cost, and the reason FlashAttention and long-context tricks exist. Two words is cheap; ten thousand words is not.
Quick check (3 questions)
1. What is a single entry of the scores matrix S = Q · Kᵀ?
The dot product of one query row and one key row — “how well does word i's question match word j's advertisement?”
2. Why divide the scores by √d_k before softmax?
Dot products grow with dimension; without scaling, softmax would saturate (all-or-nothing) and gradients would vanish. √d_k keeps the scores in a gentle range.
3. The final output for “cat” is a weighted mix of what?
The Value (V) rows, weighted by “cat”'s attention row. Output = Weights · V.
