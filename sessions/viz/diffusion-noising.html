Forward Diffusion: Adding Noise Step by Step

What am I looking at? A tiny 8×8 "image" of a simple shape. Drag the timestep slider and watch the clean picture slowly dissolve into random speckle. That is the forward diffusion process: at each step it mixes in a little more noise, following a fixed recipe (the schedule). By the last step there is nothing left but static. A diffusion model is trained to run this backwards — to turn the static back into a picture.

The image as it gets noised

Each cell = one pixel. Formula per pixel: xt = √ᾱt · x0 + √(1−ᾱt) · ε, where ε is fixed random noise.

What to notice

  • Noise is added gradually by a fixed schedule. Early steps barely change the image; later steps swamp it. The schedule (cosine vs linear) decides how fast the signal gives way to noise — cosine keeps signal alive longer near the start.
  • The whole path is one closed-form equation: xt = √ᾱt·x0 + √(1−ᾱt)·ε. You never have to step through all t values — you can jump straight to any timestep.
  • At t = T the signal fraction is essentially 0, so the image is pure Gaussian noise — every trace of the original shape is gone.
  • Training reverses this: the model learns to look at a noisy image and undo one step at a time, turning random static back into a clean picture. That reverse walk is how diffusion models generate images.
