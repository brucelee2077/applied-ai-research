1 · WHAT IS ITWhat is "report finalization," and why does it need its own week?✓
🧭
What this assumes: you finished Week 11's IsoFLOP sweeps for both the Dense and MoE models and used scipy.optimize.curve_fit to fit power laws to each. No new JAX or LaTeX experience needed — we build it up here.

The problem: a lab notebook is not a report

Over the last six weeks you ran dozens of training jobs, saved loss numbers, and fit curves. That is real evidence — but it is scattered across notebooks, scripts, and JSON files. If you handed someone your raw folder today, they could not answer one simple question in thirty seconds: at a given compute budget, does the Dense model or the MoE model reach a lower loss?

Today's new "tool" is not a JAX API. It is the discipline of turning finished experiments into a document that could survive peer review. That discipline has three concrete parts:

1. Overlay two frontiers on one figure

From Week 10–11 you have a compute-optimal frontier for the Dense model: pairs of (compute C, best-possible loss L*) that came from fitting L(C) = E + a·C^b to the minima of your IsoFLOP parabolas. Week 11 repeated the exact same pipeline for the MoE model. Today you plot both frontiers as two lines on the same log-log axes. Whichever line sits lower at a given C is the more compute-efficient architecture at that budget.

2. Make the figure report-grade, not screen-grade

High-fidelity here means: log-log axes (a good power-law fit looks like a straight line), labeled axes with units, a legend distinguishing Dense vs. MoE, the fitted exponent annotated right on the plot, and 150+ DPI or vector output — not a quick plt.plot() you'd only ever see on your own screen.

3. Give the report a fixed skeleton, written in LaTeX

A rigorous report has a shape: Abstract, Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion. LaTeX is a typesetting language that separates your content from the layout, and gives you automatically numbered equations, figures, and references — the same reason arXiv papers use it instead of a word processor. Overleaf is a browser-based LaTeX editor, so there is nothing to install.

⚠️
Nothing architecturally new gets built this week — no new model, no new JAX code. Every number you write in prose must trace back to a saved artifact (a JSON or CSV file) you can point to. A scaling-law report is judged on reproducibility, not on the plot looking pretty.
📚
Official docs (optional): Matplotlib · Seaborn · scipy.optimize.curve_fit · Overleaf LaTeX guide · Chinchilla (2203.15556) · Switch Transformer (2101.03961)

4 · HOW & WHYThe rules of a report-grade figure, and why the report matters✓

The rules

  • Every fitted power law is L(C) = E + a · C^b (Chinchilla "Approach 2"). E is the irreducible loss floor, a and b come from scipy.optimize.curve_fit on your IsoFLOP parabola minima.
  • Plot on log-log axes: taking logs of both sides turns L - E = a·C^b into a straight line, log(L-E) = log(a) + b·log(C). A curved line in log-log space means a bad fit.
  • Every figure needs: axis labels with units, a legend when more than one series is plotted, the fitted exponent annotated on the plot, and DPI ≥150 or vector (PDF/SVG) output.
  • Before overlaying Dense and MoE, re-check that C was computed the same way for both — see the gotcha below.
✅
ax.loglog(C, L_dense, label="Dense"); ax.loglog(C, L_moe, label="MoE", ls="--")
Two lines, one figure, one x-axis of compute C. Whichever line is lower at a given C is the more compute-efficient architecture at that budget.

Why it matters

  • Reproducibility over prettiness — every number in the report text must trace back to a saved JSON/CSV, not just a claim in prose. That is what a reviewer (or interviewer) actually checks.
  • The comparison is the deliverable — a Dense-only report or an MoE-only report each tell you half the story. The overlay figure is the one artifact the curriculum explicitly requires.
  • LaTeX enforces structure — Abstract, Methods, Results, Discussion aren't decoration. They force you to separate "what we measured" from "what we think it means," which is exactly the distinction a Staff-level design doc also needs.
⚠️
Gotcha: the most common failure is silent unit drift. If Dense's C ≈ 6ND used total parameters, but MoE's C was computed from active parameters for one model and total parameters for the other, the two frontiers are not comparable on the same x-axis — even though the plot renders without error. State explicitly in Methods which parameter count (active vs. total) was used for each model's C. If MoE's C undercounts unused experts, "MoE is more efficient" could be an artifact, not a real architectural advantage.

Artifact

  • experiments/week12_jax/reports/scaling_report.tex (or Overleaf-exported source) — Abstract, Introduction, Methods, Results (Dense subsection, MoE subsection, Comparison subsection), Discussion, Limitations, Conclusion.
  • experiments/week12_jax/reports/figures/dense_isoflop_parabolas.pdf, moe_isoflop_parabolas.pdf — the per-model IsoFLOP parabola-with-minima figures.
  • experiments/week12_jax/reports/figures/dense_vs_moe_frontier.pdf — the required overlay of both compute-optimal loss frontiers on one log-log figure.
  • experiments/week12_jax/reports/figures/dense_vs_moe_Nstar.pdf and ..._Dstar.pdf — overlay figures for N*(C) and D*(C).
  • experiments/week12_jax/reports/fitted_constants.json — the exact {E, A, alpha, B, beta} for Dense and MoE that every number in the report text must trace back to.

Acceptance criteria

  • Every numeric claim in the report's prose (Abstract, Results, Discussion) matches a number in fitted_constants.json or a labeled table/figure — no orphan numbers.
  • The Dense-vs-MoE frontier figure exists, uses log-log axes, has a legend distinguishing the two curves, and both curves are visibly near-linear (confirming fit quality).
  • The report explicitly states and interprets the difference in β (data exponent) between Dense and MoE, per Week 11 Sunday's derivation.
  • The LaTeX source compiles to a PDF with zero unresolved ?? references and zero missing figure files.
  • All figures are ≥150 DPI or vector format (PDF/SVG), with axis labels including units and a legend where more than one series is plotted.
