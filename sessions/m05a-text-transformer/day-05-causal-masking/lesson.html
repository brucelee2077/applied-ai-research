Module 5a · Represent · Day 5

No Peeking
at the Future

A model like GPT writes one word at a time, left to right. When it is choosing word 3, it must not be allowed to look at word 4 — that would be cheating, because word 4 does not exist yet during real writing. Today you learn causal masking: the simple rule that blocks each token from seeing anything to its right, by hiding future scores before the softmax.

🎯 By the end, you'll be able to: explain causal (look-ahead) masking, set future attention scores to −infinity before the softmax, and say why this makes the model a valid left-to-right writer.
1 · WHAT IS ITA rule: each token sees only the past✓
🧭
What this assumes: you know attention scores every pair of tokens and passes those scores through a softmax to get weights (M3), and you have seen the decoder block (Day 4).

The rule

A causal mask (also called a look-ahead mask) says: when the model looks at position i, it may only attend to positions 1, 2, … i — never to i+1 or later. Each token sees itself and everything before it, and nothing after.

The word "causal"

"Causal" means causes come before effects. In writing, earlier words cause later words, not the other way round. A causal model respects that arrow of time: the future cannot influence the present.

How the rule is enforced

Attention builds a grid of scores: how much each token wants to attend to each other token. To block the future, you take every score for a future position and set it to −infinity before the softmax. After softmax, −infinity turns into a weight of 0. So the blocked positions contribute nothing.

🧮
scores + mask → softmax → future weights = 0

One grid, a triangle of −infinity in the upper-right, and the future disappears. Keep going 👇

2 · INTUITIONWriting a story without reading ahead✓
✍️
You write left to right

When you write a story, you only know the words you have written so far. You cannot read the ending before you write it, because you have not written it yet. Each new word depends only on the words before it.

📄
A cover sheet over the future

Imagine an exam where the next answer is printed just below. You slide a cover sheet down, revealing one line at a time. The causal mask is that cover sheet: it hides everything to the right of where you are.

In one line: the causal mask makes each token behave like a writer who can look back at what is already written but never peek at what comes next.

🎮
Writing "The cat sat on": while choosing after "cat", the model sees only [The, cat]. While choosing after "sat", it sees [The, cat, sat]. The window grows by one each step — always looking back, never forward.
⚠️
Where the analogy breaks: a cover sheet is moved by hand one line at a time, but the mask blocks the future for all positions at once, in a single grid of scores. During training the model processes the whole sentence in parallel; the mask is what still forces the left-to-right rule.

4 · MECHANISM & WHYThe −infinity trick, worked out✓

Step 1 — in plain words

Attention makes a square grid of scores: row i, column j is how much token i wants to attend to token j. To block the future, you add a mask to that grid. The mask is 0 where j ≤ i (the past and present, allowed) and −infinity where j > i (the future, blocked). Then softmax turns each row into weights, and the −infinity spots become exactly 0.

Step 2 — the mask, symbol by symbol

🧮
mask[i][j] = 0 if j ≤ i # allowed: past and self
mask[i][j] = −inf if j > i # blocked: the future
weights = softmax(scores + mask)
i = the token doing the looking (a row)
j = the token being looked at (a column)

The mask is a lower-triangular grid of 0s with an upper-triangular grid of −infinity. Adding −infinity to a score sends it to −infinity; everything else is unchanged.

Step 3 — a worked example with small numbers

Take one row of scores for token 2 (which may see tokens 1 and 2, but not token 3): raw scores [1.0, 2.0, 3.0].

  • Add the mask [0, 0, −inf] → [1.0, 2.0, −inf].
  • Softmax: e^1.0 = 2.72, e^2.0 = 7.39, e^(−inf) = 0.
  • Normalize: [2.72, 7.39, 0] / 10.11 ≈ [0.27, 0.73, 0.00].

Token 3 got weight 0 even though its raw score (3.0) was the biggest. The future is gone.

Why it matters — training and generating agree

🔗
The big picture: the mask lets the model train on a full sentence in parallel while still learning "predict the next word from the past only". Without it, position 2 could just copy the true word at position 3 and never learn anything. The causal mask is exactly what makes GPT-style models possible — and it is the only difference between a decoder and an encoder (Day 6).
