Module 5a · Represent · Day 3

Each Word Thinks
in a Private Room

Attention lets tokens share information — it is a meeting where everyone tells everyone else what they know. But after the meeting, someone has to sit down and actually think about what they heard. That is the feed-forward layer: a tiny two-step network that each token runs on its own. It widens the numbers to get more room to think, filters them, then narrows back down.

🎯 By the end, you'll be able to: explain the position-wise feed-forward layer (Linear → activation → Linear), write it out, and say why it widens then narrows.
1 · WHAT IS ITA tiny two-layer network, run per token✓
🧭
What this assumes: you know attention lets tokens share information (M3), what a linear layer is (M4), and what an activation like ReLU does (M4).

What it is

The feed-forward network (FFN) is a small network that lives inside every transformer block. It has just three steps:

  1. Linear (expand): multiply the token's numbers by a weight matrix that makes the list longer.
  2. Activation: apply a simple filter like ReLU (keep positives, zero out negatives).
  3. Linear (compress): multiply by a second weight matrix that shrinks the list back to its original length.

The key word: position-wise

"Position-wise" means the FFN runs on each token by itself. It uses the same weights for every token, but each token is processed alone — no token looks at any other token here. All the mixing between tokens already happened in attention. This step is just private thinking.

Why widen then narrow

The middle is bigger than the input — usually 4 times bigger. If a token's list is 512 numbers long, the FFN expands it to 2048, filters, then compresses back to 512. The wider middle gives the model more room to combine features before packing the answer back into the original size. Keep going 👇

2 · INTUITIONA private room with a big whiteboard✓
🚪
Each word gets its own room

After the meeting (attention), each word walks into a private room to think. No one else is there. It works alone, using the same room rules as every other word.

📋
A big whiteboard, then a small note card

Inside the room is a big whiteboard (the wide middle). The word spreads its ideas out, crosses out the ones that do not help (the filter), then copies the final answer onto a small note card to carry out (compress back down).

In one line: the feed-forward layer is each token's private thinking step — spread out on scratch paper, filter, then write the answer back in the original size.

🎮
Attention decides which words to combine. The feed-forward layer decides what to do with the combined information. Two different jobs, side by side in every block.
⚠️
Where the analogy breaks: real rooms are separate places, but in the FFN every token uses the exact same weights — the same room and the same whiteboard rules. It only feels private because tokens never look at each other during this step.

4 · MECHANISM & WHYTwo matrices and a filter✓

Step 1 — in plain words

Take one token's list of numbers. Multiply it by a first weight matrix to get a longer list (expand). Run every number through ReLU: keep it if it is positive, replace it with 0 if it is negative (filter). Multiply the result by a second weight matrix to get back to the original length (compress). Two matrix multiplies and one filter — that is the whole layer.

Step 2 — the formula, symbol by symbol

🧮
h = ReLU(x · W1 + b1)
y = h · W2 + b2
x = the token, shape (d_model,) e.g. 512 numbers
W1 = expand weights, shape (d_model, d_ff) e.g. 512 × 2048
h = the wide hidden list, shape (d_ff,) e.g. 2048 numbers
W2 = compress weights, shape (d_ff, d_model) e.g. 2048 × 512
y = the output, shape (d_model,) e.g. 512 numbers

The input and output are the same length (d_model), so the FFN slots neatly into the block. The middle length d_ff is the wide part, usually 4 × d_model.

Step 3 — a worked example with small numbers

Say d_model = 2 and d_ff = 4, and a token is x = [1, −1].

  • Expand: x · W1 + b1 gives 4 numbers, say [2, −3, 0.5, −1].
  • ReLU filter: keep positives, zero the rest → [2, 0, 0.5, 0].
  • Compress: [2, 0, 0.5, 0] · W2 + b2 gives 2 numbers again, say [1.3, −0.4].

The token went in as 2 numbers, spread out to 4, got filtered, and came back as 2 numbers — a new, "thought-about" version of itself.

Why it matters — the muscle of the transformer

🔗
The big picture: the FFN holds most of a transformer's weights — often two thirds of them. Attention decides who talks to whom; the FFN is where the model actually stores and applies most of what it has learned. In real models the activation is often GELU or SwiGLU instead of ReLU, but the widen-filter-narrow shape is the same.

The staff lens — one silent failure, one trade-off

The FFN is the model's memory, and its power comes entirely from one small nonlinear step in the middle — and from how wide you make that middle.

⚠️
Failure mode (silent): the activation goes missing and the FFN collapses to one linear layer. The whole point of ReLU(x · W1) · W2 is the ReLU in the middle. If you drop it (or leave a placeholder that just passes numbers through), you get (x · W1) · W2, which is mathematically the same as multiplying by one single matrix. Nothing crashes — the shapes are identical and the code runs — but two layers are now doing the work of one, so the model quietly loses most of its capacity and plateaus at a worse loss you cannot explain from the numbers. A senior engineer in code review confirms a real nonlinearity sits between the two linear layers, because two stacked linears with nothing between them are secretly just one.
⚖️
Trade-off: d_ff width buys capacity but dominates the compute and memory bill. The middle size d_ff (usually 4 × d_model) is where most of the model's parameters and matmul FLOPs live. Making it wider gives the model more room to store and combine features, but it grows the parameter count and the per-token compute faster than almost any other knob. In a design review the FFN width is the main lever for trading model quality against training cost and inference latency — which is also why sparse mixture-of-experts replaces one wide FFN with many, activating only a few per token.
