Module 22a · Generate · Day 4

GANs

The VAE and VQ-VAE both learned by rebuilding the input — reconstruction was the teacher. A GAN throws reconstruction away entirely. Instead it sets up a game between two networks: a generator that turns random noise into an image, and a discriminator that tries to tell real images from the generator's fakes. They train against each other. When the generator finally wins — its fakes fool the discriminator — those fakes look real to us too. That is generation by competition.

🎯 By the end, you'll be able to: explain the generator/discriminator game and why it needs no reconstruction loss, read the minimax objective and say what each network is trying to do, and name the two headline failure modes — mode collapse and training instability — plus the trade-off GANs make versus a VAE.
1 · WHAT IS ITTwo networks playing a game✓
🧭
What this assumes: you know a decoder can turn a code into an image (Days 1–3), and you know a classifier outputs a probability (from the earlier foundations). A GAN puts a decoder-like network and a classifier-like network head to head.

The two players

A GAN (generative adversarial network) has two networks. The generator takes a random noise vector and turns it into an image. The discriminator looks at an image and outputs one number: the probability that this image is real, not fake.

The game

The two goals are opposite. The generator wants to make fakes so good the discriminator says "real". The discriminator wants to catch every fake. This tug-of-war is what adversarial means — they are adversaries. Each one's progress is the other's problem, so both are forced to keep improving.

No reconstruction anywhere

This is the big break from Days 1–3. There is no "rebuild this exact image" loss. The generator never even sees a real image — it only sees the discriminator's verdict on its own output. It learns purely from the signal "you fooled the judge" or "you did not".

Why it can look so sharp

Because the only pressure is "fool a judge who is looking for fakes", the generator cannot get away with a blurry, safe average (which a discriminator instantly flags as fake). It is pushed toward crisp, convincing detail. That is why GANs were famous for razor-sharp faces. The cost, as you will see, is that this game is hard to keep balanced. Keep going 👇

2 · INTUITIONThe counterfeiter and the detective✓
💵
The counterfeiter (generator)

A forger prints fake banknotes. At first they are terrible and easy to spot. Every time the detective catches one, the forger learns what gave it away and prints better ones next time. The forger never sees a real bill's secret recipe — only the feedback "caught" or "passed".

🕵️
The detective (discriminator)

The detective studies real bills and the forger's fakes, and gets sharper at telling them apart. As the fakes improve, the detective must find subtler tells. Both get better together — until the fakes are so good the detective can only guess.

In one line: a forger and a detective push each other to improve, and when the forger finally wins, the fakes are indistinguishable from real — that is a trained GAN.

The end goal is a stalemate: the discriminator can do no better than a coin flip (50/50), because the fakes are as good as real. At that point the generator has learned to produce believable images from nothing but noise.

⚠️
Where the analogy breaks: real forgers copy a fixed target. Here both players are learning at once, so the "target" keeps moving. That moving target is exactly why GANs can be unstable to train — more on that in the mechanism.

4 · MECHANISM & WHYThe minimax game, worked out by hand✓

Step 1 — the two jobs, in plain words

Write D(x) for the discriminator's probability that image x is real, and G(z) for the image the generator makes from noise z. The discriminator wants D(real) near 1 and D(G(z)) near 0. The generator wants D(G(z)) near 1 — it wants its fakes called real.

Step 2 — the objective

🧮
minG maxD E[ log D(x) ] + E[ log(1 − D(G(z))) ]
# D pushes this UP: raise D(real), lower D(fake)
# G pushes this DOWN: it wants D(G(z)) high, so log(1−D(G(z))) small

Symbols: D(x) = probability a real image is called real. D(G(z)) = probability a fake is called real. The discriminator maximizes the whole expression; the generator minimizes it. That opposite push on one shared objective is the minimax game.

Step 3 — one scoring round, worked out

Say on one batch the discriminator scores a real image at D(real) = 0.9 and a fake at D(G(z)) = 0.3. Its loss (it wants real→1, fake→0) is the negative of the objective:

🔢
D_loss = −[ log D(real) + log(1 − D(fake)) ]
     = −[ log 0.9 + log(1 − 0.3) ]
     = −[ (−0.105) + log 0.7 ]
     = −[ −0.105 + (−0.357) ] = 0.462

The generator, meanwhile, uses the non-saturating loss −log D(G(z)) = −log 0.3 = 1.204. That is large, so the gradient pushes hard to raise D(G(z)) toward 1 — i.e. make the fake look more real. As the generator improves, D(G(z)) climbs and its loss shrinks.

⚠️
Silent failure — mode collapse. The most notorious GAN failure: the generator discovers one output (or a few) that reliably fools the current discriminator, and produces only that — ignoring the noise input entirely. Every sample looks nearly identical. The loss can look totally healthy, because fooling the judge is all the loss measures; it never rewards variety. You only catch it by looking at the samples. Fixes: minibatch discrimination, unrolled/Wasserstein objectives, or spectral normalization.
⚖️
Trade-off — sharpness vs stability & coverage. GANs buy the sharpest, most realistic samples of the three models — no blurry averaging. You pay with a training process that is genuinely hard: two networks chase a moving target, so it can oscillate or collapse, and there is no likelihood number to tell you how well you are doing (you need proxy metrics like FID). A VAE trains stably and gives you a likelihood bound, but its samples are blurrier and it can never quite match GAN crispness.
🔗
The big picture: across this module you met three answers to "how do I generate?" — the VAE (rebuild through a sampled cloud), the VQ-VAE (rebuild through discrete tokens), and the GAN (win a game with no rebuilding at all). Next module, diffusion offers a fourth answer that keeps GAN-level sharpness while training as stably as a regression.
