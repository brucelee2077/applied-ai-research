4 · HOW & WHYWhy blank-page recall, not recognition, is the mechanism✓

The rules

  • Recognition is "yes, this looks right, I remember writing this" — it happens automatically when you re-read your own code and confirms familiarity, nothing more.
  • Recall is rebuilding the same formula on a blank page, from memory, with no notebook open — this is what a live technical screen actually demands.
  • Recognition survives until the interviewer changes one variable (swaps in GQA, changes batch size, asks about a different context length). Recall survives it, because it was never tied to the artifact in the first place.
  • The fix is procedural, not motivational: close the notebook first, derive on paper, and only reopen the notebook afterward to check your work against it.
✅
Close laptop → derive on paper → open laptop to check.
Not: open laptop → read code → nod → close laptop. That order proves nothing under interview pressure.

Why it's the right mechanism for this weekend

  • Your artifacts are proof, not topics — the screen tests the same arithmetic-intensity and roofline reasoning that justified your Pallas kernel, so the kernel is live evidence you can apply the concept, not just recite it.
  • The curriculum signals where to spend the marginal hour — the Scaling Book links are tagged [primary]; NeetCode and SWE-bench are tagged [support]. That labeling is a direct instruction about weighting, not a suggestion to treat every link equally.
  • Rest is part of the mechanism, not a break from it — 22 weeks of continuous building plus a live outreach campaign is real cognitive load, and recall performance drops on fatigue. Resting first is what makes the blank-page derivation actually work.
🔗
Causal chain: you built kernels using arithmetic-intensity reasoning → a technical screen will ask you to reproduce that same reasoning live → recognition (re-reading code) fails the moment one assumption changes → recall (blank-page rebuild) survives every follow-up.
