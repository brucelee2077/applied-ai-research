# Learning Experience Gate

## Purpose

This gate checks whether a lesson makes the learner want to continue.

It is not a tone check. It is a learning-barrier check.

## Bad pattern

```text
Prerequisites
→ definition
→ formula
→ mechanism
→ staff lens
→ artifact
```

This is efficient but often cold.

## Desired pattern

```text
Human situation
→ curiosity
→ familiar analogy
→ tiny concrete example
→ mechanism
→ visual/evidence
→ artifact
→ staff depth
```

## Required checks

A foundation lesson should answer:

1. Why should I care before I know the math?
2. What human experience makes this idea familiar?
3. What fear or confusion is being reduced?
4. What curiosity hook makes me want to keep reading?
5. What mental picture comes before the first formula?
6. Does the analogy guide the whole lesson, not just one paragraph?
7. Does the lesson stay warm during the technical sections?
8. Does staff-depth feel like empowerment rather than pressure?
9. Does the artifact feel like discovery rather than homework?

## P0 Learning Experience Failures

Mark P0 if:

- opening feels task-like or checklist-like,
- the first technical term arrives before a mental picture,
- foundation lesson begins with engineering abstraction instead of human intuition,
- analogy is only decoration and not the narrative spine,
- staff/frontier relevance appears before the learner is oriented,
- the lesson is technically correct but makes a beginner want to leave.

## P1 Learning Experience Failures

Mark P1 if:

- opening is okay but middle becomes dry,
- artifact feels like homework rather than exploration,
- staff lens is useful but too abrupt,
- curiosity is present but weak,
- bilingual warmth is missing.

## Experience + Staff Depth Balance

Do not dumb down the lesson.

Use this progression:

```text
curiosity first
rigor second
staff depth third
artifact always
```

The lesson should say, implicitly:

```text
This is not scary.
This is useful.
You can understand it.
Now let's go deep.
```
